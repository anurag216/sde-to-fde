# First-month career experiment

Alpha 1.0 is successful only if it improves the primary learner's engineering capability and remains engaging enough to use. The business idea is not the first experiment.

## Operating rhythm

Aim for 4–5 short sessions per week rather than marathon studying.

A normal week should contain:

- 2 practical engineering missions
- 2 DSA reps, usually Easy → Medium difficulty
- any active-recall reviews that become due
- one short reflection: what can I now explain or implement that I could not explain or implement cleanly before?

The adaptive roadmap can change this mix when evidence shows a more important gap. Do not preserve a schedule merely to satisfy the schedule.

## Day 1 baseline

Complete the diagnostic without studying first. Use the platform's hints only when genuinely stuck. The baseline should record what you can currently do, not what you can research during the assessment.

Save a progress export after the baseline.

## Week 1 acceptance

We should see evidence that the learning loop itself works:

- the learner voluntarily returns for more than one session
- at least three real mission attempts exist
- at least one coding mission is solved with deterministic tests
- at least one open-ended engineering answer is evaluated
- the learner can attach at least one new formal CS/SWE term to an idea they already understood practically
- hints feel like coaching rather than answer generation

If the learner is already bored, fix the experience before adding more curriculum.

## Week 2 acceptance

We should see reduced dependence on immediate help:

- at least one problem is solved after an initial failed attempt
- assistance level is not monotonically increasing across every challenge
- code is being written/revised in the environment rather than copied from an external AI
- the learner can explain time/space complexity for common hash/set/queue operations in ordinary engineering scenarios
- at least one backend/data concept moves from vague intuition to an explicit design explanation

## Week 3 acceptance

We should see retention rather than short-term completion:

- at least two delayed reviews become due
- the learner re-solves them from fresh evidence rather than opening an old solution
- at least one review interval expands after okay/easy recall
- a weak or hard recall is allowed to shorten the interval without being treated as failure
- the skill profile has real retention evidence rather than `unknown` everywhere

## Week 4 acceptance

We should see early next-role movement:

- complete one staged boss mission through all three stages
- produce at least one architecture/design answer that explicitly states invariants, failure modes and tradeoffs
- complete at least eight DSA reps across the month without letting DSA dominate the curriculum
- compare career-readiness evidence across coding, backend/data, distributed/production, system design, AI, FDE and technical communication
- identify which career track currently deserves more weight based on both interest and observed performance

## What counts as improvement

Evidence of improvement includes:

- fewer hints for a concept on delayed recall
- clearer decomposition before implementation
- faster recognition of appropriate data structures
- being able to name concepts such as idempotency, backpressure, optimistic concurrency, readiness, tail latency, prompt injection boundaries, or partial failure after encountering the underlying engineering problem
- more precise explanations of why a design is safe or unsafe
- better debugging hypotheses and use of evidence
- passing code tests with fewer blind edits
- transferring a concept to a different scenario

## What does not count

Do not treat these as meaningful career progress by themselves:

- XP
- streak length
- number of cards opened
- number of AI messages sent
- number of hours spent in the app
- finishing the 52-week map faster than planned
- memorizing terminology without being able to apply it

## Month-one decision

At the end of four weeks, answer three questions:

1. Am I voluntarily using this instead of avoiding traditional studying?
2. Can I demonstrate concrete engineering abilities that were weaker one month ago?
3. Is the adaptive sequencing targeting my real gaps rather than making me complete generic material?

If the answer is no to any of these, change the learning system before expanding the business/product surface.
